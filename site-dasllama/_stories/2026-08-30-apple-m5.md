An M5 Max arrived at the end of August and became the main box the same day. dasLLAMA
had never run on it. Everything below happened in the week that followed.

## What a new chip is, to this engine

dasLLAMA does not ship kernels tuned for a chip. It ships kernel *families* - several
ways to compute the same GEMV, attention or norm - and a tuner that races them on the box
it is running on, then writes the winners into a sidecar. So "supporting the M5" is not a
port. It is letting the races run and looking hard at every place where the winner changed.

Three things changed.

**The GPU tensor units.** The M5 GPU exposes tensor units through the Metal 4 tensor API.
We wrote the tensor-side twin for each matrix family and let the race decide, per family,
whether the tensor form or the simdgroup form wins on this box. On the M5 Max the tensor
twins took every K-quant matmul family, the bf16 and q8 matmuls, the MoE matmuls and the
attention QK matmul. The crown is a runtime knob in the sidecar, not a class default: a
box that did not race keeps the simdgroup form, and a box that raced and lost keeps it too.

**The CPU core layout.** The M5 Max has no efficiency cores. It has 6 "Super" cores and 12
"Performance" cores, all compute. Our CPU lane had learned, on the M4, to exclude the
efficiency tier from the pool; on the M5 that rule would have thrown away a third of the
chip. The pool now spans both tiers, and decode parks the slower tier. Both core gates were
verified live during the sweep, on both boxes.

**The other modalities.** The vision towers and the speech-to-text encoders serve on the GPU
on the M5 the way the text path does. Nothing new in the design here - the same Metal rails
- but each one had to be re-raced and its crowns re-minted.

## What it measured

The full M5 Max board was re-minted on 2026-08-30 and is on the
[board](https://daslang.io/dasllama.html) (pp512 / tg128, Metal, llama-bench protocol), from
`modules/dasLLAMA/performance/records/m5.json`:

| model | das pp512 / tg128 | llama.cpp pp512 / tg128 |
|---|---|---|
| gemma-4-E2B Q8_0 | 9074.3 / 161.3 | 7413.4 / 137.0 |
| Qwen3.8-27B Q4_K_M | 913.9 / 27.8 | 752.1 / 25.4 |
| Qwen3-30B-A3B Q4_K_M | 3752.9 / 165.7 | 3422.4 / 137.0 |
| gpt-oss-20b mxfp4 | 3995.1 / 162.7 | 3476.6 / 149.0 |

The other modalities on the same box and day: the gemma-4-12B image encode 7.1 ms against
the reference's 95 ms; the Qwen2.5-Omni window ViT 106 ms against 118; whisper
large-v3-turbo transcribing jfk in 140 ms against whisper-cli's 153 on the same GPU.

The M4 board rows were refreshed alongside (`m4.json`, 2026-08-31), including Canary-Qwen on
its new Q8_0 serving artifact: jfk in 307.6 ms on the M5 and 512.9 on the M4, beside the
NeMo torch-MPS reference's 704.7 and 1076.5. On the long gb1 clip the das row reads 6484 ms
beside the reference's 4383 - the one gap left on long audio, and the next dig. The M1 board
stands unchanged as the long-term record; its Canary rows retire with the old decoder
artifact.

## Why it was fun

Because nothing about it was a port. Every kernel that had been tuned for one generation
got raced again on the next, and the outcomes were not predictable from the spec sheet: some
winners stayed, some flipped, and a few families needed a new form written before the race
had anything worth crowning. Watching an engine re-tune itself to a chip that did not exist
when the engine was written - and finding the handful of places where it could not, and
fixing those - is the part of this project we would do for free.
